Integrability of Cauchy problem for Shramm’s circle patterns

Abstract.

I will report on our recent result with Anton Izosimov, where we have shown that the Cauchy problem for Schramm circle patterns is Arnold-Liouville integrable. I will try to explain all the terms in the above phrase, so no prior knowledge is assumed.

16 Oct 2026 (Friday, 3pm BST)
Federico Accossato Politecnico di Torino
Transcendence by Design: Continued Fractions and Diophantine Approximation

Slides

Abstract.

Who first suspected that some numbers cannot be roots of any polynomial with integer coefficients? Probably Euler, in the 18th century, but it was only in 1844 that Liouville exhibited the first explicit transcendental number. Since then, two lines of research have flourished: proving the transcendence of celebrated constants such as $\pi$ and $e$, and building new transcendental numbers from scratch. For this second goal, continued fractions have proved to be a remarkably effective tool.

In this talk, we will revisit some striking properties of continued fractions and their deep ties with Diophantine Approximation, and then see how these ingredients yield explicit examples of transcendental numbers. If time allows, we will also take a look at recent developments extending these techniques to higher-dimensional continued fractions and to other settings.

23 Oct 2026 (Friday, 3pm BST)
 Alex Heaton
Lawrence University
Generic Rigidity and the Grassmannian.

 We characterize the generic rigidity of bar-joint frameworks in all dimensions. The conditions are constructed explicitly from the graph alone, independently of any placement, and are checked by exchange rules on Young tableaux. By following paths in a directed acyclic subgraph of the line graph, we build multisets of red and blue tableaux, called balancing conditions. Then generic rigidity is determined by counting if every semistandard tableau appears the same number of times red as it does blue.
